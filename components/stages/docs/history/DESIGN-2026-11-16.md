# Simulator Stages: design

Working name. Started 2026-11-16 from [FFVII-Modding issue 767](https://github.com/CaitGPT/FFVII-Modding/issues/767),
"Add background variety to the VR arena". An AI (Claude) is building it. **Nothing here has run in the game yet.**

## The owner's idea

Each region has a boss combat assignment (its classified intel) that sits in an arena. Use those six arenas as stages
for Chadley's combat simulator.

## Approach: stream the stage to the simulator (owner, 2026-11-16)

The owner wants every stage selectable wherever the simulator is opened, so every stage is streamed in.

The simulator's stage is not a separate map. Each continent's world holds one streaming volume, labelled `COMMON_VR`,
that streams the stage level `0000-COMON_VR` into the open world at the volume's `TargetLocation`: far off the map and
up in the air. The fight itself (territory, cameras, navigation helpers and 264 named points that enemy scripts use)
is part of that level and stays where it is.

When a course is confirmed, the mod loads the chosen stage's own levels as new instances, placed so that the stage's
fight centre at ground height (its *anchor*) lands on the simulator's centre. Then it hides the simulator's floor,
blocks, spotlights and cubes. After the visit it unloads the instances and shows the simulator's look again.

| Step | What | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stream | The stage's levels appear around the fight | `LevelStreamingDynamic.LoadLevelInstance` with a position, made by the native helper (`native/`); Lua writes the requests to a file |
| dressing | The simulator's own look is gone | Lua: hide five actor classes and two streaming levels, and the simulator's sky when the stage brings one |
| release | Everything goes back | Lua: `SetIsRequestingUnloadAndRemoval` on each instance |

The helper is needed because UE4SS 3.0.1's Lua cannot make that call (its `bool` out-parameter breaks the argument
stack). It is assembled from the parts of Junon Colosseum's helper that took live testing to get right: the
game-thread check, the loader-idle check and the one-job-per-frame gate (`tools/make_helper_source.py`). It ties the
mod to UE4SS 3.0.1.

**The other route, kept as an option (`route = move`):** a stage in the open world can instead have the simulator
moved onto it by writing its ground position into the volume's `TargetLocation`. That needs no helper and loads
nothing extra, but works only on the stage's own continent. It is the first thing to try in the game, because it is
the smaller change.

The level lists are a first cut from static data (`tools/select_levels.py`): every level that places something
within 120 m of the arena, plus the terrain levels of the same sub-area. The arenas other than the final battle's are
parts of whole dungeons and regions, so a stage brings that much of its surroundings with it. Junon and the Temple of
the Ancients are over a gigabyte of level data as selected and will need trimming in the game. Left out on purpose:
collision and navigation levels (the simulator keeps its own flat floor), low-spec twins, music, alternate times of
day, and Gimmick and Physics levels (interactable and story objects), except the final battle's, which hold its ground.

Radii and unlocks are estimates: half the boss territory's wall where it has one; chapter progress after the boss's
chapter for story bosses; a stand-in flag for Gilgamesh. The story-boss anchors use the territory actor's own height,
because the ground under them is not read yet.

## How stages are unlocked and chosen (owner, 2026-11-16)

- **Stages unlock.** Beating a region's boss (its classified intel) unlocks that region's arena. Beating certain
  story bosses unlocks that boss's arena. The final battle's stages unlock when the game is finished.
- **The player picks after selecting a course.** The picker lists the stages that course can use, with the
  simulator's own stage first. The pick is remembered per course (`choices.txt` beside the mod) and reused until
  changed. Confirming the course applies it.
- **A course can use a stage when** the stage is unlocked, is big enough for the course, and can be reached from
  where the simulator was opened. `data/courses.json` gives each course the clear, flat radius it wants around the
  centre (12 m for ordinary rounds up to 28 m for Phoenix; research section 5), and each stage has a `radius_m`.
- **Every stage is reachable anywhere** when streamed. With `route = move`, an open-world stage is reachable on its
  own continent only.
- **Left alone:** the eight Shinra Manor simulator courses (owner, confirmed 2026-11-17). The eleven tutorial
  courses and the three courses the Training Dummy mod adds have the stages (owner, 2026-11-17): 70 courses in scope.
- **Nothing in the save changes** and no game file is replaced.

The picker is the game's own choice window (`UEndMenuAPI.ShowMessageWindow`), opened when a course is selected
(`OnCourseMenuPressedItem`) or on F7 for the highlighted course (`picker = key`). Junon Colosseum uses that window in
the field and in battle; on top of the simulator's menu, beside the game's own confirmation, it is untested. If the
two clash, the fallback is the key, or the stage shown and cycled on the course's info panel.

The menu exposes a course's title, not its key. Courses are matched by title: the English title from the tables,
and the title in the player's language through `UEndMenuAPI.GetString` on the course's text id.

## What is known and what is not

Known from the game's files:

- Every stage's centre and the levels around it; the final battle's ten sets and their switch volume.

- The volume, its label, its `TargetLocation` per world, and that the property is `BlueprintReadWrite`.
- The game itself streams these same stage levels at a second offset for a cutscene (`EV_COMON_2010`), so offset
  streaming of them works.
- Each arena's centre, ground height, wall size and boss.
- The simulator's floor is at the level's z = 0 with the party 5.9 m from the centre and the enemy 4.7 m the other way.

Not known until the game is free ([PROBE-PLAN.md](PROBE-PLAN-2026-11-16.md)):

1. Whether the game reads `TargetLocation` each time the simulator starts, or once.
2. Whether the open world is streamed in around the arena when the party arrives from far away.
3. How the simulator's flat floor collision sits on uneven ground (Quetzalcoatl's arena slopes most).
4. Whether enemies move properly: the simulator's baked navigation stays at its old place, the arena has its own.
5. Whether anything native to the place joins in: wildlife, a nearby camp, the boss's own territory.
6. Lighting: the stage's spotlight level and the sky switch, and what the region's time of day does.
